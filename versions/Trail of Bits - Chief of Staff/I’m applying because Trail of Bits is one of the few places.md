What's an operational or organizational challenge that you think most companies handle poorly, but could transform how they work if done well? When explaining your answer, be specific about what makes the current approaches inadequate and include concrete examples from your experience. Use analogies or comparisons from other domains (like sports, cooking, or nature) if they help illustrate your point.

Company mission and vision statements are a great example. The executive team or the marketing team or the board gets together and spend time throwing corporate speak slogans around and as a group, reducing them to sentences made of English words that are grammatically correct and devoid of real meaning. They then get added to a page on the website and to some decks and marketing slick sheets, and none of the people in the company doing the work know they even exist. 

The issue isn't that the people making this mistake aren't smart or well intentioned. It's that this is really difficult to do correctly. It takes a very iterative process that gathers real feedback from real people doing real work, then requires one or two brave people to craft that into words that mean something. (Writing by committee is torturous and ineffective.)

When a company mission and vision actually mean something it inspires the people who work there. Customers feel the difference.

Tell us about a leader or organization you deeply admire. What specifically do they do that makes them excellent?

For several years before the pandemic I volunteered in a community organization with an incredible leader named Jeremiah Morgan. He is a lawyer, at that time he was the chief counsel for the Missouri Supreme Court, and in our community organization he lead 10 local groups of several hundred people each. 

Jeremiah has incredible energy. It didn't matter if we were talking about budgets or audits (things which he didn't know much about but was always interested to learn), staffing and assignments (which he loved discussing because he loves people), or organizing activities (his favorite part, because he loves sharing things he cares about with people in the community, a natural marketer!), he always seemed able to bring curiosity and excitement to the work we did. 

I learned a lot about delegation from him. At the higher levels of any organization, delegation in essential. The work that gets done often doesn't match what was delegated, which is a challenge. Owning the delegation process, including owning and celebrating the work that gets done, was one of his strengths. In private, he would work with those he delegated tasks to so that they understood what was needed and the vision for the task, then he supported whatever ended up being done. It was hard to watch at times, when things were mangled or bungled, but everyone who worked with Jeremiah knew he cared about them as people and supported them like a good leader does.

What's something you believe about how organizations should work that most people would disagree with?


Difficult conversations are an opportunity and a gift. Much of our lives is spent trying to find comfort. (Who doesn't want to be comfortable?) Accomplishing real, important things means difficulty. Working with people requires hard conversations. The more important the situation, the higher the need for difficult conversations. 

This isn't a ticket to being a jerk or losing empathy. It's actually the opposite. The only way difficult, charged conversations work is if the leader approaches it with incredible empathy and care.
